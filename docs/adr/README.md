# Architecture decision records

ADR-001 … ADR-010 are recorded in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §19. Decisions taken during implementation:

## ADR-011 — Parquet export is produced off-device
**Decision.** The app exports CSV and JSON; Parquet is produced by `tools/analysis/lpx_reader.py` (pyarrow) from the CSV/`.lpx` files.
**Rationale.** Parquet writers for Android drag in Hadoop-sized dependencies (SOUP burden, APK size) for a research-only format.
**Consequence.** Researchers run one script; the in-app export remains dependency-free.

## ADR-012 — Fusion and rendering in Kotlin, no NDK in v1
**Decision.** The complementary filter, roll extraction and GLES renderer are Kotlin. C++/JNI is deferred until profiling shows a need.
**Rationale.** IMPLEMENTATION Phase 2: "Do not start in C++." The hot loops are allocation-free and O(1) per sample; the allocation-free property is unit-tested (REQ-SEN-031).
**Consequence.** No NDK/CMake toolchain required to build; an iOS/KMP port shares more code.

## ADR-013 — No game engine (Godot/Unity) for the passthrough renderer
**Decision.** Camera2 → `SurfaceTexture` → custom GLES 3.0 stereo renderer, as ADR-002.
**Rationale.** A game engine adds at least one frame of latency in its own pipeline, a large SOUP surface, and awkward Camera2 zero-copy access on Android. The latency budget (≤ 45 ms) is the second-ranked architectural driver.
**Consequence.** Stereo, distortion and overlays are ~1 k lines of our own GL code with pure-Kotlin geometry that is unit-tested; Godot remains an option for future gamified functional tasks rendered *inside* our passthrough, not as the host.

## ADR-014 — `.lpx` record is 22 bytes
**Decision.** The record layout listed in ARCHITECTURE §11.1 sums to 22 bytes (u32 + 2×i16 + 3×i16 + 3×i16 + u16); the text said 20. The implementation uses 22 and the architecture text has been corrected.
**Consequence.** ~1.1 MB per 20-minute session at 50 Hz.

## ADR-015 — Roll sign, mount offset and render-rotation sign are per-device data resolved empirically
**Decision.** `DeviceProfile` carries `roll_sign`, `theta_mount_deg`, `scale_error` (jig) and `render_rotation_sign` (lens-calibration screen). A `generic-android` fallback profile lets the app run on any Android 10+ phone with a gyroscope after on-device field calibration, flagged UNQUALIFIED until a jig report exists.
**Rationale.** Vendor sensor axes, camera orientation and headset mounting differ across phones; assuming any of them is how a measurement device silently reports the wrong sign.

## ADR-016 — Phone compatibility floor
**Decision.** minSdk 29 (Android 10), GLES 3.0, any back camera with a `SurfaceTexture` output, raw gyroscope + accelerometer required (`ImuCapabilities.measurementCapable`). Preview size and fps range are chosen at runtime from `CameraCharacteristics`; 30 fps phones run but are flagged for latency.
**Consequence.** Runs on the large majority of active Android phones; clinical qualification remains per model.

## ADR-017 — Landscape mounting is a calibration property, not a code path
**Decision.** Android sensor axes are fixed to the device body and never rotate with the screen. The headset holds the phone in landscape, so raw roll reads about ±90° when the head is upright; the device profile's `theta_mount_deg` (from the jig or the in-app field calibration performed *with the phone mounted*) absorbs this, and `roll_sign` is resolved empirically in the same posture. Nothing in the fusion changes with orientation. The camera image, by contrast, does depend on the phone: the renderer rotates it by whole quarter turns computed from `SENSOR_ORIENTATION` and the display rotation (`CameraOrientation.quarterTurns`), with a manual override on the lens-calibration screen.
**Consequence.** Calibration instructions insist on the mounted, landscape posture; overlays are drawn in landscape screen space and are unaffected; the HMD activity is landscape-locked while the clinician UI rotates freely.

## ADR-018 — Three device qualification tiers
**Decision.** `NONE` (no calibration: blocks clinical sessions unless research mode, flagged UNQUALIFIED on reports), `FIELD` (in-app calibration in the mounted posture, vendor rotation-vector cross-check within 2°; sessions allowed; reports state "field-calibrated, no jig report"), `JIG` (rotary-jig report within ARCHITECTURE §16 limits).
**Rationale.** "Qualified" originally meant only the jig report, which most sites will not have on day one; blocking every phone read as "my device is not supported". The field tier keeps the accuracy claim honest while letting supervised use proceed.
**Consequence.** The jig remains the requirement for any accuracy statement (REQ-SEN-040); FIELD data is labelled as such everywhere.

## ADR-019 — Visor (mono) display is the default; the stereo lens path is kept as an option
**Decision.** `HeadsetProfile.display_mode` selects between `MONO_VISOR` (default profile `phone-visor-mono-v1`: the phone is strapped on top of the head or to a cap/visor with the screen tilted toward the eyes, no lenses; one full-screen camera image, gravity-locked overlays drawn once, Mode B rotates the picture about the screen centre) and `STEREO_LENS` (`generic-open-bottom-v1`: per-eye viewports, barrel pre-distortion, chromatic correction). `PassthroughRenderer` (formerly `StereoRenderer`) implements both; the fusion, protocol engine, metrics and overlays are identical. The camera image is never stretched (`ViewMapping`), because a stretched image would show a 30° real roll as a different angle while the overlays stay exact. `rotation_fit` chooses what happens when Mode B tilts the picture: `CROP` (lens default: the picture keeps filling the eye, corners are lost to grey) or `FIT` (visor default: the whole camera frame stays visible and shrinks smoothly with the slew-limited angle, so no information is lost).
**Rationale.** The clinical kit in hand is a phone on a visor bracket, not a lens headset. Lenses buy immersion and field of view at the cost of IPD fitting, distortion tuning per headset unit, hygiene, and a fully occluded patient; for supervised balance and walking practice a visor that the patient reads like a normal display is simpler, cheaper and safer to abort (the therapist can see the screen too).
**Consequence.** Settings hide IPD and the lens-calibration wording in visor mode and show a display-alignment check instead; the pre-session text says "mount the phone on the visor"; the phone still runs landscape-locked with the same abort controls; the stereo path stays available for sites that own lens headsets, but the plumb-line acceptance check (REQ-VIS-020) and SSQ tolerability must be re-run per display mode.

## ADR-020 — The baseline is the patient's error against true vertical; the operator flow is three steps with defaults
**Decision.** (1) The baseline measurement records how far from **true vertical** (gravity) the patient holds their head when they feel upright, and how steady they are; `θ_ref` defaults to 0 and the clinician's "use current posture as midline" is an optional, audited override. (2) The clinical picture (severity, balance, assistance, fall risk) is recorded with conservative defaults (`Baseline.defaultFor`) when the baseline is captured, and edited only if the patient differs; it never gates a session. (3) The flow is register (name, age, push direction) → measure baseline (one tap, auto-saved, result stated in words: "12.3° to the LEFT") → session (protocol, mode and gain defaulted; safety attestation is one explicit "Confirm all" over a visible list; contraindications default to absent; SSQ defaults to "no symptoms"; assistance level from the baseline). (4) Single-phone operation: with the patient view open the first block, and each block after a completed rest, starts by itself after `session.auto_start_delay_s` (countdown drawn in the view; Volume Up starts at once; a touch during the countdown restarts it rather than aborting), and the view closes itself when the session ends.
**Rationale.** The previous flow required a ten-field clinical form before any measurement, a therapist-set midline before the capture, a separate save tap, 8 + 16 selectors before every session, and a "Start" button on a phone that was by then strapped to the patient's head. On hardware it produced "baseline missing" confusion (a capture rejected for too little valid data looked like a completed step) and could not start a session at all without a second screen. The measurement device's job is to state the patient's error plainly and compare every session against it.
**Consequence.** Safety semantics are unchanged: every attestation is still an explicit tap, a present contraindication still blocks, abort by touch/Volume Down/clicker still applies from the first running block, unqualified phones still block the baseline with a direct link to calibration. Reports keep `θ_ref` and who set it; a non-zero midline is shown as a chip.

## ADR-021 — The baseline error is entered, not measured; the sensor zero is an optional tool
**Decision.** The patient's baseline tilt error (degrees, + right) is entered by the operator in the disease details (`Baseline.headDeviationDeg`) and is the primary input of every session: `SessionSpec.baselineErrorDeg` becomes a constant, slew-limited picture rotation (`RenderState.staticOffsetDeg`) that counters it in every visual mode; Mode B head-roll compensation adds on top. The former "measure the baseline" step becomes an optional **sensor zero**: a short recording whose average posture becomes `θ_ref`, so a session measures sway and instability relative to the patient's own posture. It is not a clinical metric and never gates a session. The flow is register → disease details → session.
**Rationale.** Product decision by the clinical owner: the clinician knows the patient's error from assessment and wants the visual input corrected by that number; the sensor's job is stability, not diagnosis. It also removes a step that on hardware produced "baseline missing" confusion.
**Consequence.** Reports still compare against `Baseline.measured` when a sensor zero exists and say so when it does not; the entered error is printed with the session. The rotation sign is verified on the display-alignment screen like every other per-device sign (ADR-015).

## ADR-022 — Faithful full-screen picture; the applied tilt fades over sessions
**Decision.** (1) The camera quad uses the GL texture convention and lets the SurfaceTexture transform do the orientation; the earlier vertical flip showed the world upside down and, once "fixed" with a 180° turn, mirrored. A left–right **Mirror** switch and **Flip 180°** remain on the display-alignment screen for the odd device, with a three-point check (upright, right hand on the right and text readable, plumb line on a vertical edge). The stored orientation override moved to a new key so old values do not carry over. (2) The preview size is chosen to match the landscape screen's aspect at or above its resolution (then full-HD 16:9, then 720p), so the visor shows a full, sharp picture. (3) Exposure and white balance stay automatic by default (`visual.lock_exposure`), because the patient walks through changing light. (4) Every session records `applied_tilt_deg` (DB v3). `TiltScheduler` proposes the whole entered error for the first session and removes `visual.tilt_fade_step` (default 15 %) of it per session thereafter; an operator override carries forward; the session-setup card shows "picture tilt this session" with a slider under Adjust.
**Rationale.** The clinical owner's model: the patient must see the real world as it is, full screen, and be weaned off the correction gradually so that recovery, not dependence, results.
**Consequence.** The orientation must be confirmed by eye once per phone (the check screen says how); reports print the applied tilt next to the gain.

## ADR-023 — Camera orientation is read off the SurfaceTexture transform, not assumed
**Decision.** Camera2 has the compositor rotate preview buffers so they come out upright in the phone's natural (portrait) orientation; a SurfaceTexture consumer receives that rotation inside `getTransformMatrix`, not in the pixels. The renderer now decodes the rotation the camera service applied from that matrix on every frame (`CameraOrientation.frameworkTurns`), and turns the picture by `display rotation + applied rotation − sensor orientation` quarter turns, so the world is upright and left stays left whether or not the service rotated the stream. The drawn aspect follows the total number of turns, which is what made a quarter turn look "compacted" before. The operator's override is now an extra half turn on top of the automatic value (Flip 180°), stored under a fresh key.
**Rationale.** On the development phone the old rule (sensor − display) left the picture a quarter turn out because the stream was already rotated by the service; assuming either convention breaks on the other kind of phone, reading the matrix works on both.
**Consequence.** A phone's camera stream is logged once per start with the matrix, the decoded turns and the mirror flag; the display-check screen keeps Flip 180° and Mirror for anything the decode cannot know (a camera mounted backwards).
